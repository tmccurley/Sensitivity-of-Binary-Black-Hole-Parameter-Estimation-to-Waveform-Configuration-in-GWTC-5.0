Where do waveform models disagree?

An interactive comparison of how two gravitational-waveform models estimate black-hole binary properties. Explore where the results agree, where they shift, and how strongly those differences depend on the event and physical quantity.

Welcome

This site compares parameter estimates produced with IMRPhenomXPHM-SpinTaylor and IMRPhenomXPNR for 18 binary-black-hole signals in the GWTC-5.0 catalog. It is designed to help readers see whether conclusions about a source change when the waveform configuration changes.

How to use the site

Choose an event and, optionally, a parameter. The red bar follows your selected event through the charts. Hover over marks for exact values.

What “event” means

An event is one detected gravitational-wave signal, named by its date and UTC time; for example, GW240525_031210.

What “parameter” means

A parameter is a source property inferred from the signal, such as chirp mass, mass ratio, effective spin, or luminosity distance.

Interpret with openness. This is an active scientific question. A visible model difference is evidence of sensitivity, not by itself proof that one model is correct, that another is wrong, or that every future event will behave similarly. Sampling variation, event selection, prior assumptions, and waveform physics all matter.

References and further reading

These sources provide the catalog context, public data, and statistical ideas behind the comparison.

  1. GWTC-5.0: An Introduction to Version 5.0 of the Gravitational-Wave Transient Catalog - an accessible overview of the catalog and its scientific setting.
  2. GWTC-5.0: Methods for Identifying and Characterizing Gravitational-wave Transients - details of the detection and characterization methods.
  3. GWTC-5.0 Data Release Documentation - the public catalog and parameter-estimation data used as the source material.
  4. Cédric Villani, Optimal Transport: Old and New - background on Wasserstein distances.
  5. Jianhua Lin, “Divergence Measures Based on the Shannon Entropy” - the foundational Jensen-Shannon divergence reference.
